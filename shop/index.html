The code

Scan it, pick the street you’d fix, and the shirt remembers.

Every shirt in the run carries a different code on its back. The first vote cast through it ties that shirt to one street and one proposal. From then on, anyone who scans your back sees the change you asked for.

Why a shirt

A poster stays on one lamppost. A shirt walks around.

We started with City Edit codes on posters. A poster only reaches the people on that block. A shirt goes where you go: the community board meeting, the block party, the line at the bodega.

Each scan adds a little community signal about what a street could be. Enough of it, drawn to scale, is something a planner can act on.
