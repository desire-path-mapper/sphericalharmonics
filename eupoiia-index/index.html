James Watson

American molecular biologist, geneticist, zoologist and Nobel Laureate (1928–2025)

Read moreCollapseWikipedia

James Dewey Watson (April 6, 1928 – November 6, 2025) was an American molecular biologist, geneticist, and zoologist. In 1953, he and Francis Crick co-authored an academic paper on DNA entitled, "Molecular Structure of Nucleic Acids: A Structure for Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid". Published in the scientific journal Nature, the paper proposed the existence of the double helix structure of the DNA molecule, having built on research by Rosalind Franklin and Raymond Gosling. In 1962, the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine was awarded to Watson, Crick, and Maurice Wilkins "for their discoveries concerning the molecular structure of nucleic acids and its significance for information transfer in living material".

Yemen

country in West Asia

Read moreCollapseWikipedia

Yemen, officially the Republic of Yemen, is a country in West Asia. Located in southern Arabia, the country includes the Socotra Archipelago and borders Saudi Arabia to the north, Oman to the northeast, the Arabian Sea to the east, the Gulf of Aden to the south, and the Red Sea to the west. It also shares maritime borders with Djibouti, Eritrea, and Somalia across the Horn of Africa. Covering 455,503 square kilometres (175,871 sq mi), with a coastline of approximately 2,000 kilometres (1,200 mi), Yemen is the second-largest country on the Arabian Peninsula by both area and population. Sanaa is its constitutional capital and largest city. Yemen's population is estimated at 34.7 million, predominantly Muslim Arabs.

Each card represents two people or groups, living or dead, from any period: nations, empires, companies, organizations, movements, dynasties. Pick the one you think has done more for the common good: swipe the card left or right, or use the buttons. Swipe up for Skip. On a keyboard, the left and right arrows pick, = or space is a tie, and the down arrow chooses Skip. Every pick moves both ratings by standard Elo (K = 32), so beating a highly rated contender counts for more than beating an unknown one; a tie counts like a draw in chess.

The contenders come from Wikidata, chosen by how many Wikipedia language editions and sister sites cover them. That makes them recognizable, and it also inherits Wikipedia's biases toward the English-speaking, Western, recent and male. Coverage decides who is eligible, never anyone's rating: everyone starts at 1500. Names, descriptions and pictures come from Wikidata, Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons, free images only; each picture links to its file page for the author and license.
